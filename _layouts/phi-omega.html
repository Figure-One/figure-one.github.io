Phi and Omega Functions

The phi (φ) and omega (ω) functions are mathematical functions often used in number theory.

Phi Function (φ)

The phi function, also known as Euler's totient function, counts the number of integers up to a given integer n that are relatively prime to n. It is denoted as φ(n).

For example, φ(9) = 6 because the integers 1, 2, 4, 5, 7, and 8 are all relatively prime to 9.

Omega Function (ω)

The omega function counts the number of distinct prime factors of a given integer n. It is denoted as ω(n).

For example, ω(18) = 2 because the prime factors of 18 are 2 and 3.
